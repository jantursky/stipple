Stipple draws dithers. A dither turns a smooth tone into a pattern of ink and paper dots, the way old print and early displays did. In Stipple the pattern comes from a small engine, and anything in Compose can use it: a background, a button, a chart column, a photo, a line of text.

It runs on Android, iOS, JVM desktop and wasmJs from one `commonMain` source set, and has no Material dependency.

Three ideas carry everything:

- A **field** says how much ink each point wants. It is a pure function of position and time.
- A **pattern** is a threshold map. A cell is ink when the field's tone is above its threshold: Bayer 2, 4 and 8, blue noise, or your own.
- A **theme** holds the ink and paper colours, an optional palette, and the cell size.

On top of the engine there are modifiers (`dither`, `ditherProgress`, `ditherImage`, `ditherFill`, `ditherMask`) and ten components: `DitherButton`, `DitherBarChart`, `DitherGrid`, `DitherGauge`, `DitherStatCard`, `DitherProgressBar`, `DitherText`, `DitherAvatar`, `DitherImage` and `DitherBackground`. Components are static unless you pass `animated = true`, and animation stops under reduced motion.
