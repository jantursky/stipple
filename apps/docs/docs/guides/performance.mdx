description: What a dither costs per frame, and how to keep it out of composition.

## Draw phase only

The animation clock is snapshot state that only the draw block reads. A frame redraws the element and recomposes nothing. The tests count recompositions while a dither animates and require the count to stay at zero.

Do the same in your code. Pass progress as a lambda, so the read happens while drawing:

```kotlin
val progress by animateFloatAsState(target)
Box(Modifier.ditherProgress({ progress }))
```

Reading `progress` in the composable body would recompose on every frame.

## Cell count is the cost

The cell renderer samples the field once per cell and draws neighbouring cells of one colour as a single rectangle. The cost grows with the number of cells, which is width times height over the square of the cell size. As a guide:

| Surface | Cell | Cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 x 100 dp button | 3 dp | about 1,100 |
| 360 x 800 dp phone screen | 3 dp | about 32,000 |
| 360 x 800 dp phone screen | 1 dp | about 288,000 |

The renderer coarsens the grid above 90,000 cells. For big surfaces use `DitherRenderer.Auto`, which switches to the shader past 20,000 cells where it can.

Animated dithers redraw every frame. A static dither is drawn once and cached by Compose like any other content.

## Images

`ditherImage` reads the content back once, dithers it on a background dispatcher and caches the result. The previous result stays on screen while a new one computes. Ordered dithering is cheapest. Floyd-Steinberg and Atkinson diffuse error and cost more per cell, which only shows on very large images.

## Measuring on a device

`scripts/frame-bench.sh` measures the gallery's `bench` page, three large animated dithers at a one-dp cell, with each renderer (`--entry bench-blue-noise` and `--entry bench-dots` measure the same load with the blue-noise pattern and with dot cells), and writes the frame times to `results.csv` and a `summary.md`.

```sh
scripts/frame-bench.sh android --seconds 10 --repeat 3   # an attached phone or a running emulator
IOS_DEVICE_UDID=<udid> scripts/frame-bench.sh ios        # a physical iPhone
```

On Android the frames come from `dumpsys gfxinfo framestats`: the time from the intended vsync to the frame being completed, for every frame drawn after a three second warm-up. The summary gives p50, p90, p95 and p99, the share of frames that took longer than one refresh interval, and the gap between the best and worst of the repeated runs, which is the noise to compare differences against. Compare renderers on one device in one session, and use `--repeat 3` or more. An emulator renders in software, so its numbers show the direction, not what a phone does.

## Measured

A Samsung SM-G991B (Galaxy S21) on Android 15 at 120 Hz, `--seconds 10 --repeat 3`, median run by p95, at commit `caeef48`. The refresh interval is 8.3 ms, so every row is over budget at 120 Hz. Against 60 Hz (16.7 ms) only the blue-noise shader run stays inside the budget at p95; the other shader runs have p95 near 21 ms.

| Page | Renderer | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | p95 spread ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bench` (Bayer 4, squares) | cells | 175.0 | 184.4 | 189.6 | 6.8 |
| `bench` | shader | 14.5 | 21.0 | 23.1 | 9.0 |
| `bench-blue-noise` | cells | 221.9 | 235.0 | 237.8 | 2.6 |
| `bench-blue-noise` | shader | 11.2 | 12.6 | 15.3 | 3.9 |
| `bench-dots` | cells | 367.6 | 473.0 | 477.8 | 57.9 |
| `bench-dots` | shader | 15.4 | 21.7 | 23.9 | 9.5 |

At a one-dp cell the cell renderer is an order of magnitude slower than the shader, and dots cost it the most, since each dot is a separate circle. Blue noise costs the shader nothing measurable over Bayer: its texture lookup is within the run-to-run spread.

## What is not measured yet

Frame times on a low-end Android device and on a physical iPhone have not been measured; the phone above is a high-end one. The gallery's frame meter shows the time between frames, not the cost of rendering.
